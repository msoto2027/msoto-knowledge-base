# Programs & Routines

> Learning how to create workouts that actually fit my life.

One thing I am realizing while getting back into training is that having a workout plan makes it much easier to stay consistent.

A good program does not have to be complicated. The best routine is one I can realistically follow and improve over time.

## Why Have A Workout Routine?

A routine helps me:

- Know what to do when I walk into the gym
- Stay consistent
- Track progress
- Avoid randomly changing workouts
- Build confidence

Without a plan, it is easy to either do too little or try to do too much.

## What Makes A Good Routine?

A good workout routine should:

- Match my current fitness level
- Fit my schedule
- Include exercises I enjoy
- Allow enough recovery
- Give me a way to improve

The goal is creating something I can repeat and build on.

## Common Workout Structures

### Full Body Workouts

Full body workouts train multiple muscle groups during each session.

This can be a great option when:

- Getting back into fitness
- Training fewer days per week
- Building consistency

### Upper And Lower Splits

This separates workouts into upper body and lower body days.

Example:

Upper body:
- Chest
- Back
- Shoulders
- Arms

Lower body:
- Legs
- Glutes
- Core

### Push Pull Legs

This separates workouts based on movement patterns.

Push:
- Chest
- Shoulders
- Triceps

Pull:
- Back
- Biceps

Legs:
- Quads
- Hamstrings
- Glutes

## How Often Should I Train?

The best schedule depends on my goals and availability.

A realistic starting point:

- 2 to 3 days per week when rebuilding consistency
- 3 to 5 days per week for more regular training

More workouts are not always better. Recovery matters too.

## Creating My Own Routine

Things I want to think about:

### My Goal

Am I trying to:

- Build strength?
- Gain muscle?
- Improve fitness?
- Build a healthier habit?

### My Schedule

How many days can I realistically commit to?

### My Experience

Am I learning movements or ready for more advanced training?

## Things I Want To Avoid

I want to avoid:

- Changing programs every week
- Following workouts that do not fit my goals
- Copying advanced routines too soon
- Training without enough recovery

A simple routine followed consistently will beat a perfect routine I cannot maintain.
