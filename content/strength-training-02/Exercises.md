# Exercises

> Learning the main strength training movements and understanding what exercises are meant to improve.

When I first started strength training, I focused a lot on finding the "best" exercises.

I'm learning that there is not one perfect exercise.

The best exercises are the ones that fit my goals, feel comfortable to perform, and allow me to improve over time.

## Understanding Exercise Selection

When choosing exercises, I want to think about:

- What muscles or movements I want to train
- My current experience level
- Available equipment
- How comfortable I feel performing the movement

A good workout does not require dozens of exercises.

A few well-chosen movements can be enough.

## Main Movement Patterns

Most strength exercises fall into a few basic movement patterns.

Learning these patterns helps me understand how workouts are built.

## Squat Pattern

Squat movements focus on lower body strength.

They usually train:

- Quadriceps
- Glutes
- Core

Examples:

- Bodyweight squat
- Goblet squat
- Back squat
- Leg press

## Hinge Pattern

Hinge movements involve bending through the hips while keeping control of the spine.

They usually train:

- Hamstrings
- Glutes
- Lower back

Examples:

- Deadlift
- Romanian deadlift
- Kettlebell swing

## Push Movements

Push exercises involve moving resistance away from my body.

They usually train:

- Chest
- Shoulders
- Triceps

Examples:

- Push ups
- Bench press
- Shoulder press
- Dumbbell press

## Pull Movements

Pull exercises involve bringing resistance toward my body.

They usually train:

- Back
- Biceps
- Rear shoulders

Examples:

- Rows
- Pull ups
- Lat pulldowns
- Cable rows

## Carry Movements

Carry exercises involve moving while holding resistance.

They can improve:

- Grip strength
- Core stability
- Overall strength

Examples:

- Farmer carries
- Loaded carries

## Core Exercises

Core training focuses on building stability and control.

Examples:

- Planks
- Dead bugs
- Leg raises
- Cable rotations

The core is involved in many strength movements, not just direct abdominal exercises.

## Compound vs Isolation Exercises

### Compound Exercises

Compound exercises use multiple joints and muscles.

Examples:

- Squats
- Deadlifts
- Bench press
- Rows

Benefits:

- Train multiple muscles
- Build overall strength
- Efficient for workouts

### Isolation Exercises

Isolation exercises focus on a specific muscle.

Examples:

- Bicep curls
- Tricep extensions
- Leg curls
- Calf raises

Benefits:

- Target specific areas
- Add extra training volume
- Help balance workouts

Both types can have a place depending on my goals.

## Beginner Exercise Priorities

When getting back into training, I want to focus on:

- Learning basic movements
- Improving technique
- Building consistency
- Slowly increasing difficulty

I do not need the most advanced exercises right away.

Mastering the basics creates a stronger foundation.

## Exercise Progression

An exercise can become more challenging by:

- Adding resistance
- Increasing repetitions
- Improving control
- Changing variations

Progress does not always mean moving to the hardest version.

Sometimes improving the basics is the best next step.

## Exercise Safety

Before adding difficulty, I want to make sure:

- My form is controlled
- The movement feels comfortable
- I understand the exercise
- I can recover properly

If something causes pain or feels wrong, I should adjust instead of forcing it.
