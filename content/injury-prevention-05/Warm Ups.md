# Warm Ups

> Learning how to prepare my body before training instead of jumping straight into the workout.

When I first started working out again, I didn't always understand the purpose of warming up. I thought it was just something people did before a workout because they were supposed to.

Now I'm learning that a good warm up helps me prepare physically and mentally for the movements I'm about to do.

## Why Warm Ups Matter

A warm up helps my body transition from being at rest to being ready for exercise.

A good warm up can help me:

- Increase blood flow
- Prepare my muscles and joints
- Improve movement quality
- Practice the exercises I will be doing
- Feel more confident before starting

A warm up is not about getting tired before the workout. It is about getting ready for the workout.

## What Makes A Good Warm Up?

A good warm up should gradually prepare my body for the activity ahead.

It usually includes:

- Light movement
- Mobility exercises
- Activation exercises
- Practice sets

The warm up should match what I am about to train.

## General Warm Up

A general warm up can include:

- Walking
- Light cycling
- Easy movements
- Dynamic stretches

The goal is simply to raise my body temperature and start moving.

## Exercise Specific Warm Up

A more specific warm up prepares me for the exercises I plan to do.

Examples:

Before squats:

- Bodyweight squats
- Hip mobility movements
- Light practice sets

Before upper body training:

- Shoulder movements
- Light presses
- Band exercises

This helps me practice the movement before adding more resistance.

## Dynamic vs Static Stretching

This is something I have been learning more about.

### Dynamic Stretching

Dynamic stretching involves moving through a range of motion.

Examples:

- Leg swings
- Arm circles
- Walking lunges

This is commonly used before workouts.

### Static Stretching

Static stretching involves holding a stretch for a period of time.

Examples:

- Holding a hamstring stretch
- Holding a quad stretch

This can be useful after workouts or during recovery.

## How Long Should A Warm Up Be?

A warm up does not need to take a long time.

For most workouts, around 5 to 15 minutes can be enough depending on:

- The workout intensity
- My experience level
- How my body feels that day

Some days I may need more preparation, especially if I feel stiff or haven't moved much.

## Common Warm Up Mistakes

Things I want to avoid:

- Skipping the warm up completely
- Doing a warm up that makes me tired
- Using the same warm up for every workout
- Rushing through movements

The goal is preparation, not exhaustion.
