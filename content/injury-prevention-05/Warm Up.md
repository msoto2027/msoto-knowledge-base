# Warm Up

> Learning how to prepare my body before workouts so I can move better and train with more confidence.

When I first started working out again, I sometimes wanted to skip the warm-up and get straight into the workout.

I'm learning that warming up is not just extra time before exercising.

It helps prepare my body and mind for the work ahead.

## Why Warm Ups Matter

A good warm-up can help me:

- Increase body temperature
- Prepare my muscles and joints
- Practice movement patterns
- Improve focus
- Feel more ready for training

The goal is not to tire myself out before the workout.

The goal is preparation.

## What Makes A Good Warm Up?

A warm-up should match the workout I am about to do.

A general warm-up may include:

- Light movement
- Dynamic exercises
- Mobility work

A specific warm-up focuses on the movements I will perform during training.

Example:

Before squats:

- Bodyweight squats
- Hip mobility
- Lighter practice sets

## General Warm Up

A general warm-up increases movement and prepares my body.

Examples:

- Walking
- Light cycling
- Jump rope
- Easy cardio

This can be useful before many types of workouts.

## Dynamic Movement

Dynamic movements involve moving through a range of motion.

Examples:

- Leg swings
- Arm circles
- Walking lunges
- Bodyweight squats

These movements help prepare my body for activity.

## Mobility Before Training

Mobility focuses on my ability to move through a range of motion.

Areas I may focus on:

- Hips
- Shoulders
- Ankles
- Spine

Mobility needs can vary from person to person.

The goal is improving movement, not forcing flexibility.

## Warm Ups For Strength Training

Before strength workouts, I want to prepare for the movements I will perform.

Examples:

Before lower body training:

- Light cardio
- Bodyweight squats
- Hip movements
- Practice sets

Before upper body training:

- Arm movements
- Shoulder mobility
- Light pressing or pulling

- [[Strength Training]]

## Warm Ups For Cardio Training

Before cardio, I want to gradually increase my effort.

Examples:

Before running:

- Walking
- Light jogging
- Dynamic leg movements

Before cycling:

- Easy pedaling
- Gradually increasing resistance

Starting slowly helps my body adjust.

Related topic:

- [[Cardio Training]]

## Common Warm Up Mistakes

Things I want to avoid:

- Making the warm-up harder than the workout
- Doing only static stretching before intense movement
- Rushing through preparation
- Copying a warm-up without understanding why I am doing it

A warm-up should help my workout, not become the workout.

## Creating A Simple Warm Up

A basic warm-up can look like:

1. Light movement
2. Dynamic exercises
3. Practice movements
4. Start the workout

The exact warm-up can change depending on the day.
