# Cardio Types

> I'm learning that cardio isn't just running. There are plenty of ways to improve endurance, and finding something I enjoy makes it much easier to stay consistent.

One of the biggest mindset shifts I've had is realizing that cardio can look different for everyone. Some people enjoy long runs, while others prefer cycling, swimming, or simply walking more throughout the week.

The important part is finding an activity that gets you moving and that you'll actually want to come back to.

## Walking

Walking is one of the easiest ways to improve cardiovascular fitness.

Some benefits:

- Easy on the joints
- Great for beginners
- Can be done almost anywhere
- Easy to increase over time

I've learned not to underestimate how effective regular walks can be.

## Running

Running increases your heart rate quickly and can improve endurance over time.

It's also higher impact than walking, so I'm reminding myself to increase distance and pace gradually instead of trying to do too much too soon.

## Cycling

Cycling is a great lower-impact option.

It can be done:

- Outdoors
- On a stationary bike
- At different resistance levels

It's a good choice if I want to challenge my cardiovascular system while reducing stress on my joints.

## Swimming

Swimming works both the heart and muscles while being gentle on the body.

It's especially useful for people who want a full-body workout with minimal impact.

## Rowing

Rowing combines cardio with strength by working both the upper and lower body.

It can be a great alternative if I want something different from running or cycling.

## HIIT

High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) alternates between short periods of hard work and recovery.

While HIIT can be effective, I've learned that it isn't required to get good cardio results.

Building a consistent routine is usually more important than always choosing the hardest workout.

## Choosing What's Right For Me

When deciding what type of cardio to do, I'm asking myself:

- Do I enjoy it?
- Can I do it consistently?
- Does it fit my current fitness level?
- Will I still want to do it next month?

Finding something I enjoy is more valuable than forcing myself to do something I hate.
