# Common Mistakes

> These are some of the mistakes I'm trying to avoid while rebuilding my cardio routine.

Getting back into cardio has reminded me that it's easy to let motivation take over and forget that progress takes time.

These are some habits I want to be aware of as I continue training.

## Starting Too Fast

It's tempting to jump right into running long distances or doing intense workouts.

I've learned that starting slower usually leads to better long-term results.

Building endurance gradually helps reduce burnout and makes it easier to stay consistent.

## Thinking Cardio Has To Be Hard

I used to believe every cardio workout needed to leave me exhausted.

Now I know that's not true.

Easy and moderate workouts are still effective and play an important role in improving fitness.

## Ignoring Recovery

Cardio still places stress on the body.

Recovery helps my muscles repair and gives my body time to adapt.

Rest days aren't a sign of laziness. They're part of the process.

## Comparing Myself To Others

It's easy to compare my pace, distance, or endurance to someone else's.

The problem is that everyone has different goals, experience, and starting points.

The only comparison that really matters is how I'm improving over time.

## Being Inconsistent

Doing one amazing workout won't make much difference if I stop exercising for weeks afterward.

Building a routine I can maintain is much more valuable than pushing myself for a few days and quitting.

## Forgetting To Enjoy It

I've realized that I don't have to love every workout, but I should enjoy the overall process.

Trying different activities has helped me stay motivated and avoid getting bored.

## Things I Want To Remember

- Progress takes time.
- Walking counts.
- Recovery matters.
- Consistency beats perfection.
- Every workout is another step forward.
