# Calories & Macros

> This is one of the topics I used to overcomplicate. The more I've learned, the more I realize that understanding the basics is enough to make better nutrition choices.

When I first started looking into nutrition, I saw people talking about calories, macros, cutting, bulking, and tracking everything they ate. It felt overwhelming.

I'm learning that I don't need to know everything to make progress. Understanding a few core concepts goes a long way.

## What Are Calories?

Calories are simply a way of measuring how much energy food provides.

My body uses calories to:

- Keep my heart beating
- Power my brain
- Support daily activities
- Fuel workouts
- Recover after exercise

Whether my goal is to lose weight, maintain my weight, or gain muscle, calories play an important role.

## What Are Macronutrients?

Macronutrients, or "macros," are the nutrients my body needs in larger amounts.

There are three main macronutrients:

### Protein

Protein helps support:

- Muscle growth
- Muscle recovery
- Feeling full after meals

Foods high in protein include:

- Chicken
- Fish
- Eggs
- Greek yogurt
- Cottage cheese
- Beans
- Tofu

Since I'm strength training, I'm trying to include a good source of protein with most meals.

### Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are my body's preferred source of energy.

They're especially helpful before workouts because they give me energy to train.

Examples include:

- Rice
- Potatoes
- Oats
- Bread
- Pasta
- Fruit

I've learned that carbs aren't something to fear. They're an important part of a balanced diet.

### Fats

Healthy fats support many important functions in the body.

They also help keep meals satisfying.

Examples include:

- Avocados
- Nuts
- Seeds
- Olive oil
- Peanut butter
- Fatty fish

Like everything else, balance is key.

## Do I Need To Track Macros?

Not necessarily.

Some people enjoy tracking everything they eat.

Others prefer using general guidelines and focusing on balanced meals.

Right now, I'm focusing on eating enough protein, including fruits and vegetables, and making balanced choices most of the time.

If I ever decide to track calories or macros, I want to use them as a tool to learn more about my eating habits, not something to obsess over.

## Things I'm Learning

- Calories aren't "good" or "bad." They're simply energy.
- Protein is especially important when building or maintaining muscle.
- Carbohydrates help fuel my workouts.
- Healthy fats are an important part of a balanced diet.
- I don't need to eat perfectly to make progress.
