# Organizing System Analysis: The Metropolitan Museum of Art Online Collection

## Introduction

The Metropolitan Museum of Art (The Met) offers an extensive online collection that allows visitors to explore hundreds of thousands of artworks from around the world. The digital collection makes it possible for users to search, browse, and learn about art without visiting the museum in person. Its organizing system is designed to help users discover artworks through multiple pathways while providing detailed information about each object.

---

## Primary Resources Being Organized

The primary resources in The Met's online collection are works of art. These include paintings, sculptures, photographs, textiles, jewelry, furniture, ancient artifacts, and decorative objects from many different cultures and historical periods. Each artwork has its own page containing images, the artist's name (when known), the date it was created, the materials used, dimensions, geographic origin, department, and a detailed description. Many entries also include conservation notes, exhibition history, and related publications, giving users a complete understanding of each piece.

---

## Primary Interactions Supported

The online collection supports several important interactions.

- **Research:** Students, educators, and researchers can search for detailed information about specific artists, artworks, or historical periods.
- **Browsing:** Users can explore artworks by department, culture, geographic region, artist, object type, or time period.
- **Learning and Discovery:** Curated collections, featured exhibitions, and related works encourage users to learn about artistic movements and discover new pieces they may not have searched for directly.

These features make the collection valuable for both academic research and casual exploration.

---

## Classification System

The Met primarily uses a **faceted classification system**.

Users can filter artworks by:

- Department
- Artist
- Object type
- Material
- Culture
- Geographic location
- Date or time period
- Open Access availability

The museum also uses a **hierarchical classification system** by organizing artworks into departments such as:

- European Paintings
- Egyptian Art
- Arms and Armor
- Modern and Contemporary Art
- Asian Art

This combination makes the collection easy to navigate because users can either browse broad categories or narrow their search using multiple filters. The organizing system is effective for beginners, students, educators, and art professionals alike.

---

## Relationships Between Resources

The Met connects related resources in several ways.

- Artwork pages link to the artist's profile, allowing visitors to view other works by the same artist.
- Objects are connected through shared metadata such as culture, medium, materials, and historical period.
- Users can browse additional works within the same department or collection.
- Related exhibitions and publications provide additional context about individual artworks.
- Search results often lead users to similar objects, encouraging exploration beyond their original search.

These relationships help visitors better understand how artworks connect across different cultures, time periods, and artistic styles.

---

## Conclusion

The Metropolitan Museum of Art's online collection is an excellent example of an effective organizing system. Its combination of faceted search, hierarchical organization, detailed metadata, and interconnected resources allows users to efficiently research, browse, and discover artwork. Whether someone is conducting academic research or simply exploring art for personal interest, the collection provides an intuitive and well-structured experience that makes a large digital collection accessible to everyone.
